# Sleep Channel — Maximising RPM

*What actually moves the number, ranked by measured effect — and why sleep is
structurally better positioned than almost any other niche.*

Compiled 2026-09-26.

---

## 1. What RPM actually contains

```
RPM = (Total Revenue ÷ Total Views) × 1,000
```

**It includes, after YouTube's cut:**

| Source | Cut taken |
|---|---|
| Ad revenue | YouTube keeps **45%** |
| Channel memberships | YouTube keeps 30% |
| Super Chat / Super Thanks / Stickers | 30% |
| **YouTube Premium watch-time share** | pooled |

> **Because Premium is inside RPM, and Premium pays on *watch time* not ad
> impressions, the single biggest lever on RPM is minutes watched per view.**

Effective RPM typically lands at **40–65% of CPM** once ad-blockers, skips and
non-eligible geos are counted (TubeAnalytics Q2 2026).

---

## 2. The measured lever: watch time per view

**r/PartneredYoutube, same creator, same game, two videos:**

| Watch time | Views | Revenue | **RPM** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 minutes | 1,100,000 | $2,900 | **$2.68** |
| **49 minutes** | 216,000 | $3,800 | **$18.00** |

> **8× the watch time → 6.7× the RPM.** And the commenter's own note:
> *"one consistent thing is that **Premium pays based on watch time**."*

**This is why sleep is the right niche and not just an easy one.**

`CATEGORIES.md` measured our Tier A median runtime at **170 minutes**. A sleep
viewer is not 8× past that video's 49-minute mark — they're **3× past it**. No
other format on the platform structurally delivers this.

## 3. The levers, ranked by measured effect

### 🥇 1 · Geography — ~20–66× swing

| | CPM | RPM | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🇳🇴 Norway | $43.15 | **$19.42** | yttools 2026 |
| 🇺🇸 United States | $35.00 | **$15.75** | yttools 2026 |
| 🇬🇧 UK | $27.91 | $12.56 | yttools 2026 |
| 🇮🇳 India | $1.90 | $0.86 | yttools 2026 |
| 🇧🇩 Bangladesh | $0.65 | **$0.29** | yttools 2026 |

**Norway vs Bangladesh = 66×.** Our own live measurement (TubeTube, five
markets) gave **$0.83 CPM in Russia → $0.10 RPM → 1.38M views = $128.58/28
days** — the ~20× figure in `RESEARCH.md` §1.

**Action:** English titles/thumbnails, US-relevant framing, **upload timed to
the North American evening** — which `SCHEDULE.md` §4 already fixed at
**19:00–20:00 EST**. This is the one lever where our schedule decision and our
revenue decision are the same decision.

⚠️ Country figures vary **2–4× between sources** (MilX puts US at ~$14.67
avg CPM, yttools at $35). **Ratios are consistent even when absolutes
aren't.** Plan on ratios.

### 🥈 2 · Watch time per view — structural, we already win

Covered in §2. Nothing to change; **do not sacrifice minutes for pace.**

### 🥉 3 · Mid-roll density — *the unresolved A/B*

Two sourced strategies, both live:

| | SemideCoco (AdSense screenshot) | autotube |
|---|---|---|
| Placement | **front only — never middle, never end** | every **25–35 min**, first at minute 15 |
| Rationale | interrupting sleep destroys the product | more impressions per view |
| Revenue | *"reduces the revenue potential"* | higher |

**Our videos are 100–200 min. At 8+ minutes, mid-rolls are available** — that's
a structural advantage every short-form niche lacks. A 170-min video with
mid-rolls every 30 min carries **~6 ad slots per view**; front-only carries 1.

> **This is the single highest-value experiment we can run, and it needs no
> traffic to design** — publish matched pairs and read retention + revenue per
> viewer. Never pick one on theory.

**The mechanism that matters:** `creaticalc` — *"More impressions per view
raises your effective RPM **even when CPM stays flat.** This is the easiest
revenue lever most small channels ignore."*

### 4 · Seasonality — ⏰ **time-critical**

| Quarter | CPM vs baseline |
|---|---|
| **Q4 (Oct–Dec)** | **+80–150%** |
| Q1 (Jan–Mar) | **−30–50%** |
| Q2–Q3 | baseline |

> *"Many creators earn **50% of their annual revenue in Q4**. Plan your best
> content for October–December."*

**Today is 2026-09-26. Q4 opens in five days.**

This is the most actionable finding in the file: **a channel that exists on
Oct 1 earns ~1.8× what the same channel earns in Q1.** It argues for
compressing Wave 0–1 (`ORGANISE.md` §9) rather than planning for a calm
Q1 launch.

### 5 · Premium share — invisible, and sleep-heavy

Premium pays on watch time with **no ad required**. Sleep viewers skew toward
people who pay to remove ads *and* still need background audio overnight —
plausibly Premium-heavy. **Not measured; flagged as a hypothesis in §6.**

### 6 · Niche/advertiser category — the ceiling

| Niche | 2026 RPM | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Finance | $5–20 (MilX) · $18.40 (ytstudio survey) · $10–20+ (vpk) | ceiling |
| Education | $3–8 · $9.80 | |
| Gaming | $0.50–4 · $2.10 | |
| **Music / lo-fi / ambient** | **$0.80–2.50** | videopublishkit |
| **Sleep / healing soundscapes** | **$10.92** | OutlierKit Jun 2026 |

⚠️ **Unresolved conflict:** `videopublishkit` puts ambient at **$0.80–2.50**
(*"advertisers pay less for background-listening content because viewers rarely
click ads mid-mix"*) while OutlierKit puts sleep soundscapes at **$10.92**.

**Reading:** these measure different objects — *music/lofi channels* (pure
background, no narration, no face) vs *sleep soundscapes* (structured, often
narrated). Which supports `TEMPLATES.md` §1 and `STRATEGY.md` R2:

> **Narration is both the moat and the money.** Pure-ambient is the low band;
> narrated is the high band. Plan on the narrated band.

### 7 · Audience loyalty — secondary

Subscribers watch longer → more ad impressions → higher RPM. Which is a
restatement of §2, arrived at from the other side.

## 4. What NOT to chase

- **Medical/health adjacency** — `sleep meditation for depression · insomnia ·
  pain relief` autofills hot (`DAILY.md` §2) but health is a **sensitive
  topic** = the third inauthentic-content category (`RESEARCH.md` §2). High CPM,
  existential risk. **Don't.**
- **Shorts for revenue** — CPM $0.01–0.06 vs $3–15 long-form. Shorts are
  discovery only (`RESEARCH.md` §7 already noted Shorts hours don't even count
  toward YPP).
- **Chasing Q1 numbers down** — a Q1 dip of 30–50% is *normal advertiser
  behaviour*, not a signal your channel broke. Pre-commit to that now so January
  doesn't look like failure.
